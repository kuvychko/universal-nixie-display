# About

A project by Igor Kuvychko: a desktop display that is enjoyable as an object and useful as an
instrument, and an excuse to understand two kinds of cold-cathode tube properly.

## Sources

- John and Robin Acton, [Dekatron – The Story of an Invention](https://www.computerconservationsociety.org/resurrection/res61.htm),
  *Resurrection* 61, Computer Conservation Society.
- The National Museum of Computing, [The Harwell Dekatron / WITCH](https://www.tnmoc.org/witch).
- [Ericsson 1954 Dekatron catalogue](https://electricstuff.co.uk/dekatron.html), transcribed on
  electricstuff.co.uk.
- Industrial Alchemy, [Dekatron glow-transfer counting tubes](https://www.industrialalchemy.org/tubepage.php?item=15):
  dekatron types, speeds and fill gases.
- [Threeneurons' Dekatron Shield](https://threeneurons.wordpress.com/arduino/dekatron-shield-kit/),
  the reference A101 design for this project's operating values and circuit topology.
- Datasheets and design references are listed in the repository's
  [references](https://github.com/kuvychko/universal-nixie-display/blob/main/docs/references.md).

## Acknowledgements

The dekatron controller and power-supply boards were made by
[PCBWay](https://www.pcbway.com/), who sponsored their fabrication. Ordering was easy and
smooth, and their website gives a lot of information along the way, including a step-by-step
[walk-through of how a board is made](https://www.pcbway.com/pcb-service.html). The boards
arrived a week after the order, sealed with a desiccant pack and clearly labelled, with
clean milled edges (no break-away tab remnants to file).
